# Back Patch (Nine Spinning Skulls) - Nano Banana prompt

**Shape:** circle · **Size:** 190 x 190 mm · **Threads:** 4 · **Palette:** #5E1B1B - deep oxblood (field, keylines, eye sockets, nasal notches, skull shadow planes), #2A0508 - dark oxblood (hub ring), #F5EBD8 - bone-cream (skulls, hub crescent, motion arc), #FFF7E6 - bone-highlight (skull lit planes); black satin border via `border_black.yaml`

## Prompt

```
Flat vector illustration of a spinning wheel of skulls mid-tumble, as a large round patch. A solid deep-oxblood disc on a plain white background, white reaching all four corners, square composition; no drawn border ring. The skull ring is a strong ellipse rushing at the viewer, long axis corner to corner — never flat and front-on. Exactly nine heavy, aggressive bone-cream skulls ride it: four big near skulls lower left, five smaller far skulls upper right, each turned 40 degrees further clockwise and biting over its neighbour. Build every skull from big flat planes — cranium, cheekbone, solid jaw, heavy brow — with two big deep-oxblood sockets and one nasal notch; no teeth, cracks or fine lines. Give each skull two steps: a lighter bone-highlight plane above, a deep-oxblood shadow plane under the jaw. One thick deep-oxblood keyline rims every bone shape, keeping all nine countable. The hub is a bold layered disc — a dark-oxblood ring around a deep-oxblood core — with a bone-cream crescent of light upper left; near skulls bite over its edge. One chunky bone-cream motion arc sweeps inside the ring. Depth comes only from stacked flat shapes.

Exactly four flat colours: deep oxblood, dark oxblood, bone-cream, bone-highlight. Solid fills only — no gradients, no shading, no texture, no motion blur. Every shape large; blunt or squared ends, chunky pointed tips allowed, nothing thin or needle-like. High contrast, clean hard boundaries. No text, no lettering, no numbers.
```

## Spec

- **Subject:** DCC large back patch — Donut's Book 6 note that the back-patch skulls spin/move and there are nine heads.
- **Composition:** solid deep-oxblood disc filling the frame on white; nine heavy bone-cream skulls on a tilted elliptical ring rushing toward the viewer, long axis corner to corner (lower-left near, upper-right far) — four big near skulls, five smaller far skulls, each turned a further 40° clockwise than its neighbour and biting over it; every skull built from big flat planes with two deep-oxblood eye sockets and one nasal notch, a lighter bone-highlight plane above and a deep-oxblood shadow plane under the jaw; a bold layered hub of a dark-oxblood ring around a deep-oxblood core with a bone-cream crescent highlight upper left; one chunky bone-cream motion arc inside the ring; a bold deep-oxblood keyline rims every bone shape — depth from stacked flat tone steps, never shading.
- **Finished size:** 190 x 190 mm circle — the set's boss patch, sized 10 mm under the project's 260 x 200 mm fit box so registration margin remains.
- **Embroidery notes:** 4 threads — deep oxblood carries the field plus every internal keyline, eye socket, nasal notch and skull shadow plane; dark oxblood #2A0508 fills the hub ring; bone-cream fills skulls, hub crescent and motion arc; bone-highlight #FFF7E6 adds the lit plane on each skull. The satin border is **black** via the shared `border_black.yaml` config (the set's borders are uniform), not oxblood. At 190 mm the near skulls run ~52 mm tall with ~11 mm sockets and ~16 mm highlight planes, the far skulls ~28 mm with ~7 mm sockets, the hub is ~66 mm across, the keyline ~4 mm thick and the arc ~24 mm thick, so every region digitizes as a few clean fills — no thin satins, tiny islands, or detached teeth. **Deliberately recoloured off Scavenger's Daughter's white-on-black** so the two back-patch candidates don't read as the same patch.
- **Pipeline:** run `tools/digitize/run.sh --key-background --colors 4 --size-mm 190 --config patch_descriptions/border_black.yaml <image>`. The raster path only detects a satin border on transparent input (hence `--key-background`); `--border-color` is SVG-only and `run.sh` drops it for raster inputs, and the auto border colour is sampled from the outer band — the field colour on light patches, i.e. invisible. The shared config forces `border.mode: force_black` so the satin border is always a visible dark line.
